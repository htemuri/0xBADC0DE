## Introduction

Welcome, fellow enjoyers of ThinkPads, to the first part of *Project Phoenix*! This is an effort where I attempt to modernize an X200 with modern components unlocked by a *framework* 13 mainboard. The other day, I came across a cool [video by jvscholz](https://youtu.be/j6AXBiEdKtI?si=J_jEIWqLE7dIfF_L) about the [X210AI](https://www.tpart.net/about-x210ai/), and I fell back in love with the form factor. I remember using the X2xx series laptops in highschool. The heavy chassis, the tactile feeling of the keyboard and cursor left/right buttons, the smooth user experience of not lifting your wrist to use the trackpoint was just *chef's kiss*. In the modern age of laptops, we often compromise repairability and upgradeability for ultra-slim formfactors (👀 Apple) or maximum performance (LPDDR5). Framework is an OEM that aims to nullify these tradeoffs with their modular design and open engineering documentation files. I want to piggyback off of their work and explore the other benefits their open hardware can fruit.

In this part, we will be going over the scope of the project.

- Chassis
  - Modifying Chassis to fit the mainboard
  - Fan shroud for good thermals
- Display
  - Bezel
  - Wiring
- Battery
  - BMS
  - Pack made from 18650 cells perhaps
  - retrofit in 47+++ enclosure
- Keyboard
  - Converting custom connector to framework input cover interface
  - Get trackpoint working too
- I/O
  - Daughterboard for speakers
  - USB 3.0
  - USB C with dp-alt
  - Gigabit LAN
